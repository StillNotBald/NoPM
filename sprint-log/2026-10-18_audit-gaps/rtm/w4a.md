# RTM rows - W4-A tier-engine (R-148, R-149, R-150)

## REQUIREMENTS.md bullets

- **R-148** Approval tiers: configurable per workspace, routed to the right role, lifted by a cost threshold (audit gaps G1, G2, G7; change request assumption A3). Every governed request (change request, task or project deletion, rebaseline) carries one of five tiers (routine, normal, high_risk, emergency, strategic); the workspace policy says which role decides each tier; a cost at or over a threshold turns a normal change request into a high-risk one. Acceptance: (a) migration 0100 adds `workspaces.approval_policy` (jsonb, default `{}` = every default), its shape enforced by `nopm_approval_policy_valid` (five tier names, `approver_role` manager or admin, `turnaround_hours` 0 to 8760, `auto_approve` true on routine only, `currency_threshold`, `currency`, `emergency_retro_hours` 1 to 720), and `tier` (default `normal`, CHECK on the five names) plus `approval_deadline_at` on change requests, deletion requests and rebaselines; every new CHECK is NOT VALID then validated in a skip-on-failure block, no backfill, no `updated_at` bump; (b) defaults: routine and normal are decided by a manager or admin, high_risk and strategic by an admin only, emergency retro by an admin; a project deletion is high_risk by default (database trigger and `classify`), a task deletion and a rebaseline are normal; (c) `lib/governance/tiers.ts` holds `classify`, `approverRoleFor`, `roleMeetsTier`, `deadlineFor`, `policyOf` and `reclassifyOnEdit`; a requester may ask for `routine` (only with no cost and no schedule impact, else 422), `normal`, `high_risk`, or `strategic` (admin only, else 403), never for less than the floor; the cost threshold (default 50,000, either sign, in the policy currency) lifts normal to high_risk, and a cost in another currency than the policy names is treated as high_risk; (d) routing: `resolveWorkspaceApprovers(workspaceId, {tier, assignedApproverId})` returns admins only for an admin-level tier and managers plus admins otherwise, and an assigned approver alone when eligible; the raise paths notify that audience with `approval.requested` and the integration hub's approval cards use the same audience; an assigned approver who is below the tier's role is refused at raise (422); (e) enforcement at decision time in the route (`requireTierRole` in decide.ts for change-request and deletion decisions, the rebaseline sign-off route and the chat sign-off) as 403 `role_not_permitted` with the sentence "This request needs an admin to decide it.", and in the database: `nopm_tier_required_role` and `nopm_principal_meets_tier`, a trigger on change_requests (also: the tier can only rise, a cost edit that crosses the threshold raises it, money or dates on a routine request make it normal), the 0061 deletion trigger now tier-aware, and a trigger on rebaseline_signoffs, all P0018; (f) the Settings page gains an "Approval policy" card beside the nudge card (five rows: decided by, turnaround hours, routine-only auto-approve checkbox with a warning; cost threshold, currency, emergency window; rows stack at phone width); `GET/PATCH /api/workspace-settings` return and accept `approval_policy` (PATCH admin only, validated, merged onto the stored policy, ONE `workspace.settings_update` audit row with before and after); (g) the change-control inspector shows the tier badge, and `judgeDecision` no longer offers Approve to a viewer whose role is below the tier ("This request needs an admin to decide it"). Outcome: easier reporting (a configurable, auditable approval line) and non-code configuration. Release order: migrations 0100 and 0101 must be applied BEFORE the build serves traffic (the raise routes write `tier` and `approval_deadline_at`; the change-control page selects them). — **DONE** (wave 4, 2026-10-18; behaviour change for the owner: a PROJECT deletion now needs an admin by default, and the policy card can move any tier to either role)
- **R-149** Turnaround timers and routine auto-approve, off by default (audit gap G3; assumptions A1 and A3). Every waiting request has a deadline; the daily scheduler reminds at half the turnaround, escalates past it, and, only where the workspace admin switched it on, approves a routine request nobody decided. Acceptance: (a) the deadline is the raise time plus the tier's `turnaround_hours` (default routine 24, normal 48, high_risk 72, strategic 120; 0 means no timer) and is written at the raise with a `deadline_set` event; (b) migration 0101 creates `approval_events` (target_type, target_id, kind in deadline_set, reminder_sent, escalated, auto_approved, retro_overdue), unique per (target, kind) so a second tick the same day does nothing, RLS select-only for members, write grants revoked from the browser roles; (c) a new step of the existing daily cron (`runCron().approvals`, `lib/governance/timers.ts`; no new `vercel.json` entry) per workspace: a reminder to the tier's approvers at 50% of the turnaround (`approval.reminder`, never to the requester), an escalation to the admins past the deadline (`approval.escalated`), and ONE `approval.timer_run` audit row per workspace per run in which the timer looked at anything, with the counts; the timer never writes to the request row, so an approver's pinned version stays valid; (d) auto-approve: `nopm_auto_approve_routine(workspace, id, now)` approves a change request ONLY when `tiers.routine.auto_approve` is true, the tier is routine, it is not an emergency, it carries no cost and no schedule impact, it is still waiting and past its deadline; one transaction writes the decision (actor `system:auto-approve (policy)`, the nil-uuid system actor, no principal, `decision_rationale` stating the policy), the `auto_approved` event and exactly ONE `change_request.decide` audit row (`request_id` cron, `after.auto_approved` true); deletion requests and rebaselines are never auto-approved; the requester is notified (`approval.auto_approved`) and the hub's open cards are retired; (e) with the policy off (the default) a routine request past its deadline is escalated, never approved, and the database function is not called. Outcome: easier reporting (nothing waits unseen). Release order: migration 0101 after 0100, both BEFORE the build serves traffic. — **DONE** (wave 4, 2026-10-18; the timer runs once a day with the existing cron, so a 24 h turnaround is noticed within a day; turnaround defaults other than routine/normal are placeholders the owner can change in Settings)
- **R-150** Emergency path: ship now, approve afterwards, flagged until approved (audit gap G4; assumption A3). A manager or admin can raise an emergency change request that may be implemented at once and owes an admin's retro decision within the workspace window (default 48 h). Acceptance: (a) `POST /api/projects/:id/change-requests` accepts `is_emergency: true`; only a manager or admin may (else 403 `role_not_permitted`, nothing stored); the row gets tier `emergency`, status `emergency`, `is_emergency` true, `retro_due_at` (raise time plus `emergency_retro_hours`), no ordinary deadline, and the admins are told; (b) migration 0100 adds `is_emergency`, `retro_due_at`, `retro_decided_at`, `retro_outcome` and CHECKs that keep them consistent (flag and tier agree, status `emergency` needs the flag, the retro stamps travel together); `cr_maker_checker` allows an emergency without a decider yet, the 0092 CHECK `cr_implemented_after_decision` is relaxed for emergencies (compared with the raise, because go-live precedes the retro decision) and a new CHECK says `implemented` needs a decision or the emergency flag; (c) `nopm_implement_change_request` (same signature, one overload) accepts `emergency` as a source status; (d) the retro decision is the ordinary decide path: `nopm_decide_change_request` (same signature) accepts `emergency` and a live emergency still owing its retro decision (status `implemented`, `retro_decided_at` null), requires the emergency tier's role (admin by default), keeps four-eyes (the raiser cannot give the retro approval), stamps `retro_decided_at` and `retro_outcome`, leaves a live change `implemented` (it happened) and refuses `defer` on it; (e) flagged until decided, computed live: a pending item is awaiting retro when `is_emergency` and `retro_decided_at` is null, and overdue when also `retro_due_at` is in the past (`isAwaitingRetro`, `isRetroOverdue`); the daily timer writes a `retro_overdue` event once and notifies the admins; the digest card and the weekly report gain an "Emergency changes overdue for approval" line (`retroOverdue` in the digest data), the audit pack CSV gains `tier`, `is_emergency`, `retro_due_at`, `retro_decided_at`, `retro_outcome`, `retro_overdue`, and the change-control inspector shows an emergency note (red once overdue), decides an emergency before and after go-live, and offers "Mark implemented" on an emergency request. Outcome: easier reporting and audit evidence (an emergency leaves a visible, time-boxed exception, not a silent bypass). Release order: migrations 0100 and 0101 BEFORE the build serves traffic. — **DONE** (wave 4, 2026-10-18; the approvals queue and the review queue show `tier` and the retro columns through W4-B's pending-item work, see `rtm/w4a-openapi-notes.md` for the column names)
